\section{One-shot aggregation, then primary sources}
\label{sec:joint}

Appendix~11 aggregates a variable-importance vector that never leaves its
site.
The ten-site benchmark in Section~\ref{sec:primary-source} asks a prior
question: which of those sites carry a new concept, and which of them
would corrupt the aggregate if they were treated as another noisy copy of
the same shift.
The two designs share the upload and the target.
They do not share the failure mode.

Each site $k$ computes a local vector $V_k\in[0,1]^p$ with
$\sum_j V_{k,j}=1$, and uploads it once.
The aggregate is a convex combination
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:aggregate}
\bar V=\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k V_k,
\qquad w_k\ge 0,
\qquad \sum_k w_k=1,
\end{equation}
or a coordinate-wise median of the same uploads.
Uniform weights are $w_k=1/K$.
Sample-size weights are $w_k\propto n_k$.
Inverse-variance weights use a per-site variance estimate, floored so that
a vanishing variance cannot take the whole mass.
The column $n|S|$ is the size-and-support weight in the simulation grid.
Its gain over the uniform average is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:delta-ns}
\Delta(n|S|-\mathrm{unif})
=
\rho(n|S|)-\rho(\mathrm{unif}).
\end{equation}
Client-agreement Shapley, in the corruption table below, is a weight on
sites.
It is not Delta-Shapley.
Delta-Shapley is the feature-wise absolute difference of Shapley values
between two batches, and it appears only in the single-batch TabReD
comparison.
Rank aggregation averages within-site ranks.
Communication is $O(Kp)$ floats.
No raw row moves.
Under the simplex constraint the Matrix Bernstein bound of
Appendix~11 controls $\|\bar V-\mathbb{E}\bar V\|_2$ with a leading factor
$(\log(2p/\delta)/K_{\mathrm{eff}})^{1/2}$, and the coordinate-wise
refinement replaces the spectral norm by an $\ell_\infty$ bound that is
enough for a Top-$K$ corollary when population shares are separated by a
fixed margin.
The bound does not choose among the mean, the median, and inverse-variance
weights.
The tables do.

Throughout, $\rho$ is Spearman correlation with a stated reference.
The reference changes with the table, and the change is the point.
Centralized importance, the pooled out-of-distribution wrapper, and
whole-batch FSDS are three different targets.
A high $\rho$ against one of them is not a high $\rho$ against the others.
Selection AUC and precision at $K$ ask whether the top coordinates of
$\bar V$ cover a planted support.
They can stay perfect after $\rho$ has already fallen.
Pairwise concordance asks whether sites agree with each other.
On several grids below it moves in the opposite direction from $\rho$
against the pool.

Table~\ref{tab:fed-vimp-attack} is a weight problem, not a sample-size
problem.
Two random corruptions drop the uniform Spearman from a usable ranking to
$0.055$, and sample-size weights only to $0.156$.
The coordinate-wise median stays at $0.741$, and client-agreement Shapley
at $0.737$.
Extreme corruption and a two-site collusion are milder for the mean than
random replacement is, and the median remains the highest or the second
highest entry in every row.
A corrupted vector is not a small site.
Down-weighting small $n_k$ does not remove it.
A robust summary of the coordinates does.

Table~\ref{tab:pol-ood} is the covariate arm and the concept arm on the
same folds.
A single fold of the random-forest domain classifier already correlates
$0.848$ with the pooled wrapper; sample-size weights move that to $0.887$
and inverse-variance weights to $0.918$.
Leave-one-coordinate MMD moves from $0.891$ to $0.985$ under sample-size
weights.
Both wrappers are estimating a property of $P(X)$ that is shared across
random folds, so averaging reduces noise.
Pseudo-outcome risk does not follow them.
A single fold correlates only $0.410$ with the pooled PO-risk vector.
Sample-size weighting lowers that correlation to $0.185$.
Inverse-variance weighting returns it to $0.431$, essentially the
single-fold number.
Averaging made the concept vector worse, and a variance weight did not
repair it.
Section~\ref{sec:primary-source} is the design that explains the gap.
Random folds of one concept are what the domain classifier and MMD see.
A concept vector is large on a drifted site because the local regression
changed, and it can also be large on a site whose labels are noise,
because a flat predictor sits far from a sharp reference.
Those two uploads do not estimate the same target.
Inverse-variance weights assume that they do, and that the variance
estimate says which copy is cleaner.
Table~\ref{tab:pol-ood} is what happens when that assumption fails.

\subsection{High-dimensional corruption}
\label{sec:hidim}

The product grid crosses the number of corrupted sites $n_{\mathrm{bad}}$,
the local-support fraction $\alpha$, and the signal-to-noise ratio on the
corrupted sites.
The bad-site weight $w_{\mathrm{bad}}$ is $0$ at $n_{\mathrm{bad}}=0$ and
$1$ at $n_{\mathrm{bad}}=12$, and the variance ratio is blank at both
extremes, so those two rows are the empty-group endpoints of the grid.
Read as an inverse-variance weight, $w_{\mathrm{bad}}=0.17$ at one
corrupted site and a variance ratio of $0.44$ is the share one site
receives among twelve when its variance is $0.44$ times the clean-site
variance. Equal share would have been $1/12$.
Entries are means over five replicates.
The full grid is Table~\ref{tab:Product_HighDimResults}.
Five rows carry the reading.

Selection and rank correlation break at different places.
At full support and SNR $=0.5$, precision of the mean, the median, and
inverse-variance weights is still $1$ when every site is corrupted, while
Spearman of the mean has already fallen from $1$ to $0.719$.
The ranking gets noisier before the top set is lost.
At SNR $=0$ and twelve corrupted sites the selection AUC of the mean falls
to $0.479$ and its precision to $0$.
There is no remaining clean concept to recover.
One clean site is enough for the mean and not for the median: at
$n_{\mathrm{bad}}=11$, SNR $=0.05$, and $\alpha=1$, precision of the mean
is $0.92$ and precision of the median is $0.14$.
Thin support hurts the median before any site is corrupted.
At $\alpha=0.25$, SNR $=0.5$, and $n_{\mathrm{bad}}=0$, median precision
is $0.29$ and median AUC is $0.768$, against precision $1$ and AUC $1$ for
the mean and for inverse-variance weights.
The manuscript's statement that the median is fragile under partial overlap
is this cell, not a cell with a large $n_{\mathrm{bad}}$.

Inverse-variance weights pay for the variance ratio.
At one corrupted site, $\alpha=1$, and SNR $=0.5$, the variance ratio is
$0.44$ and the bad-site weight is $0.17$, about twice the equal share
$1/12$.
Spearman falls to $0.844$ against $0.959$ for the uniform mean, while
precision stays $1$.
The same pattern repeats down the SNR column: corrupted sites report a
smaller variance, receive more than their count share, and the rank
correlation moves before the selected set does.
Flooring the variance, which the Matrix Bernstein argument already
requires, is the condition under which this inflation stays bounded.
It does not make inverse-variance weights a detector of a bad concept.

Table~\ref{tab:fed-sim-size-hetero} is the same split with the heterogeneity
fraction in the rows.
At low size mismatch, $\Delta$ stays inside a percentage point for every
hetero value, and pairwise concordance falls from $0.339$ to $0.157$ as

Heterogeneity of the shift and disagreement among sites are not evidence
that the aggregate has failed.
They are the regime in which a size weight earns its keep, provided the
large sites are the ones whose vector estimates the pool.

The sites agree with each other and disagree with the whole.
Every aggregator returns a Spearman near $-0.20$.
There is no weight that can turn a consensus of the wrong vector into the
pooled vector.
Table~\ref{tab:num105-stab-summary} locates the defect on the split.
Under the feature-rank protocol, twenty replicates keep pairwise
concordance $0.88\pm 0.01$ and $\rho$ at $0.07\pm 0.17$ for every

aggregation problem, in which sites are noisy and the mean works.

Leave-one-site-out influence answers a narrow question: if the reference
vector is held fixed, how much does $\rho$ move when one upload is deleted.
On the random partition the aggregate is already aligned
($\rho_{n|S|}=0.889\pm 0.106$ over $45$ cells) and the largest deletion

($\rho_{n|S|}=0.243\pm 0.241$) and the largest deletion effect is $0.057$,
the same magnitude.

by $0.093$ under $n|S|$.

way.

detector has to be computed from the site's own outcome model rather than
from the change in a simplex average.

\subsection{What is delegated to the site score}
\label{sec:handoff}

The aggregation tables support four uses of $\bar V$, and they mark two
places where a better weight is the wrong repair.
First, when the uploads are noisy copies of one covariate shift, sample-size
weights and inverse-variance weights raise Spearman with the pool, and the
gain is large exactly when site sizes differ
(Tables~\ref{tab:pol-ood} and~\ref{tab:fed-sim-size}).
Second, when a few uploads are replaced by arbitrary vectors, the median and
client-agreement Shapley hold a Spearman near $0.8$ after the uniform mean
has fallen below $0.2$
(Table~\ref{tab:fed-vimp-attack}).
Third, selection of a planted support survives a large corrupted fraction
whenever one clean concept remains and the local support is not thin; rank
correlation moves earlier, and the median loses the top set under thin
support even with no corruption
(Table~\ref{tab:hidim-boundary}).
Fourth, on balanced TabReD splits, $n|S|$ is a safe default because it ties
the uniform average, not because it wins a large margin.

The two failures are not weight failures.
Pseudo-outcome risk on random WhyShift folds becomes less correlated with
its own pooled vector after sample-size weighting.
Cooking \texttt{num\_105} and \texttt{num\_184} have high pairwise
concordance and a near-zero or negative correlation with the whole, for
every aggregator, and leave-one-site-out influence stays near $0.05$
whether the aggregate is aligned or not.
Both say that the sites are not noisy copies of the reference.
A convex weight, a median, and a deletion diagnostic all assume that they
are.
The next section drops that assumption.
Sites share $P(X)$ by construction, the concept is planted on five of ten
sites, and one further site replaces labels by coin flips.
The site score is a skill-gated excess risk against a size-matched burn-in
model.
It is a weight that can be computed before $\bar V$, and it is the object
that separates a learnable concept from a consensus breaker.
The aggregation results above are what that score is being asked to protect.
