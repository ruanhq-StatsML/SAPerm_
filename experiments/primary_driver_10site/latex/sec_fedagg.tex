\section{Aggregate the distribution shift variable importance through federated aggregation}
\label{sec:FedAGG_MetaLearner}

In federated learning, model outputs are aggregated across sites in later
stages, with the aim of preserving as much information as possible.
This section asks whether FSDS, together with out-of-distribution variable
importance, can sit in that pipeline: raw rows stay local, and each site
uploads one score vector.
The section answers four questions.
When does the weight on a site change, and to what.
How the aggregate responds when individual sites are corrupted.
What the Matrix Bernstein argument actually supports.
How a shift driver is justified when many sites are present and they do
not all carry the same concept.

Federated aggregation keeps raw rows local and moves only scores or
nuisance fits.
For variable importance, \cite{wang2019interpret} computes Shapley values
for host features in vertical federated learning, while
\cite{ducange2026fedshap} constructs row-free SHAP backgrounds via
federated clustering and GANs.
\cite{bonsignori2026iflash} aggregates client-specific SHAP vectors using
accuracy and faithfulness weights instead of a flat mean.
For vertical feature selection, \cite{fu2023feast} ranks features via
conditional mutual information on statistical summaries, and
\cite{li2023fedsdg} refines this with Gini initialization and encrypted
stochastic gates.
To mitigate shift, \cite{khellaf2026fedforest} pools split statistics to
grow a federated forest whose impurity decrease approximates a pooled
forest under covariate and outcome shift, and constructs global propensity
scores via membership weights, yielding Fed-IPW and Fed-AIPW that use
cross-site treatment heterogeneity to improve overlap.
For causal effects, \cite{xiong2023federated} ships propensity summaries to
construct IPW/AIPW estimators, and \cite{vo2022bayesian} learns a posterior
over potential outcomes via Gaussian processes across sites.
\cite{khellaf2025federated} compares meta-analysis and plug-in estimators,
whereas \cite{almodovar2024pwfedavg} uses propensity-weighted FedAvg to
prevent sites with scarce treated rows from dominating.

FSDS is applied to the same task.
Rank correlations are computed for MMD-LOCO, a random-forest domain
classifier, and pseudo-outcome risk, each against a stated reference:
centralized importance, the pooled wrapper, or whole-batch FSDS.
These references are not interchangeable.
Some information loss across isolated sites is unavoidable.
For permutation variable importance and for PermuCATE, inverse-variance
weighting accounts for uncertainty at each site.
Sample-size weights and the coordinate-wise median are the other
magnitude aggregators.
The subsections below say which of these is the weight to use, using the
robustness grids, the real-data splits, and the concentration bound.

\subsection{When the weight on a site changes}
\label{sec:when-weight}

Site \(k\) uploads one vector \(V_k\in[0,1]^p\) with
\(\sum_j V_{k,j}=1\).
The aggregate is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:agg-weight}
\bar V=\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k V_k,
\qquad w_k\ge 0,
\qquad \sum_k w_k=1,
\end{equation}
or the coordinate-wise median of the same uploads.
The default is the uniform weight \(w_k=1/K\).
It is the right default when the sites are noisy copies of one shift and
are about the same size.
The weight on site \(k\) changes only in the rows of
Table~\ref{tab:weight-rule}.
Communication remains one upload of \(p\) floats per site, \(O(Kp)\) in
total.
No rule below asks for a second round.

Size is the first change.
On the \(245\)-cell grid of Table~\ref{tab:fed-sim-size}, with size in
\(\{0.1,\ldots,0.9\}\), feature correlation in \(\{0,0.15,\ldots,0.6\}\),
and heterogeneity in \(\{0,\ldots,1\}\), the gain
\(\Delta(n|S|-\mathrm{unif})=\rho(n|S|)-\rho(\mathrm{unif})\) is
\(+0.004\) in the low size-mismatch bin and \(+0.285\) in the high bin.
There the sites agree least with each other (pairwise concordance
\(0.105\)) and the size-weighted aggregates agree most with the mixed pool
(\(0.905\), against \(0.620\) for the uniform average).
Rank aggregation moves the other way, from \(0.658\) to \(0.580\).
Table~\ref{tab:fed-sim-size-hetero} splits the same grid by the
heterogeneity fraction.
At high size mismatch the gain rises from \(+0.24\) at hetero \(=0\) to
\(+0.37\) at hetero \(=1\), while \(\rho(n|S|)\) rises from \(0.893\) to
\(0.927\).
Table~\ref{tab:fed-sim-high-grid} shows that this rise is present at every
feature correlation from \(0\) to \(0.60\).
Pairwise disagreement is the regime in which a size weight earns its keep,
provided the large sites are the ones whose vector estimates the pool.

A replaced vector is not a small site.
Table~\ref{tab:fed-vimp-attack} replaces one or two client vectors.
Two random replacements leave the uniform Spearman at \(0.055\) and the
sample-size weight at \(0.156\).
The median stays at \(0.741\) and client-agreement Shapley at \(0.737\).
Client-agreement Shapley is a weight on sites.
It is not Delta-Shapley, which is the feature-wise absolute difference of
Shapley values between two batches and appears only in the single-batch
comparison of Table~\ref{tab:tabred-fsds-all}.

Inverse-variance weights are the third change, and only under the variance
bound in Assumption~(A6) below.
On ten random WhyShift folds
(Table~\ref{tab:pol-ood}), the random-forest domain classifier moves from
\(0.848\) on a single fold to \(0.887\) with sample-size weights and to
\(0.918\) with inverse-variance weights.
MMD-LOCO moves from \(0.891\) to \(0.985\) with sample-size weights.
Both wrappers estimate a property of \(P(X)\) shared across folds.
Pseudo-outcome risk does not: a single fold correlates \(0.410\) with the
pooled PO-risk vector, sample-size weights lower that to \(0.185\), and
inverse-variance weights return it to \(0.431\).
Averaging a concept vector as if it were another noisy copy of one concept
makes the ranking worse.
The concept row of Table~\ref{tab:weight-rule} is the repair: set the
weight proportional to the site score of
Section~\ref{sec:drivers}, which is zero when the local model has no skill.

The last row is a refusal.
Cooking-time \texttt{num\_105}
(Tables~\ref{tab:fed-tabred-summary} and~\ref{tab:num105-stab-summary})
has pairwise concordance \(0.884\) and site-to-whole correlation
\(-0.198\).
Uniform, sample-size, \(n|S|\), and rank all return a Spearman near
\(-0.20\).
Under a feature-rank protocol the headroom
\(\Delta(n|S|-\mathrm{unif})\) is \(+0.01\); under a random array split the
Spearman rises to about \(0.80\) and the headroom is \(0\).
The split axis is the defect.
Retuning \(w_k\) does not change it.
Leave-one-site-out influence does not find it either
(Section~\ref{sec:real-fed}).

\subsection{Robustness to corruption of individual sites}
\label{sec:pressure}

The pressure test uses many sites and asks whether the ranking of shifted
features survives corrupted centers and a lower signal-to-noise ratio.
The benchmark is a high-dimensional synthetic design rather than a small
saturated table such as IHDP.
Unless otherwise noted, local VIMP is an FSDS share, a random-forest
importance, or \(|\Delta\mathrm{corr}|\).
A dash means the variance ratio is undefined because one of the two groups
is empty, or that the cell was not computed.
The product
\(n_{\mathrm{bad}}\times\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{low}}\times\alpha\) is
Table~\ref{tab:Product_HighDimResults}, now with the inverse-variance
column included so that weight inflation can be read off the same grid.
Entries are means over five replicates.
\(w_{\mathrm{bad}}\) is the weight mass on corrupted sites.
It equals \(0\) at \(n_{\mathrm{bad}}=0\) and \(1\) at
\(n_{\mathrm{bad}}=12\).

Selection and rank correlation break at different places.
At full local support \(\alpha=1\) and SNR \(=0.5\), precision at \(K\) of
the mean, the median, and inverse-variance weights is still \(1\) when
every site is corrupted, while Spearman of the mean has fallen from \(1\)
to \(0.719\).
At SNR \(=0\) and twelve corrupted sites the selection AUC of the mean
falls to \(0.479\) and its precision to \(0\).
One remaining clean site is enough for the mean and not for the median:
at \(n_{\mathrm{bad}}=11\), SNR \(=0.05\), and \(\alpha=1\), precision of
the mean is \(0.92\) and precision of the median is \(0.14\).
Thin support hurts the median before any site is corrupted.
At \(\alpha=0.25\), SNR \(=0.5\), and \(n_{\mathrm{bad}}=0\), median
precision is \(0.29\) and median AUC is \(0.768\), against precision \(1\)
and AUC \(1\) for the mean and for inverse-variance weights.

Inverse-variance weights pay for a small reported variance.
At one corrupted site, \(\alpha=1\), and SNR \(=0.5\), the variance ratio
is \(0.44\) and \(w_{\mathrm{bad}}=0.17\), about twice the equal share
\(1/12\).
Spearman falls to \(0.844\) against \(0.959\) for the uniform mean, while
precision stays \(1\).
Assumption~(A6) floors this variance so the weight stays bounded.
The floor does not turn inverse-variance weighting into a detector of a
bad concept.
Table~\ref{tab:hidim-boundary} records the five rows that carry this
reading.
The full grid follows.

Sample-size imbalance is a different corruption.
Table~\ref{tab:federated_imbalance} reports Spearman correlation of the
aggregate with the clean aggregate under a Dirichlet allocation of site
sizes.
Uniform weights and the median stay at \(1\) for covariate shift across
the concentration grid, including the severe imbalance at concentration
\(0.05\).
Inverse-variance weights are lower on that grid, down to \(0.610\) for
concept drift at concentration \(10\).
A pool of five covariate-shift sites and five concept-drift sites
correlates only about \(0.30\) with the clean aggregate, for every
aggregator that is defined.
Size weights do not turn two kinds of shift into one ranking.
That is the gap filled by the site score in
Section~\ref{sec:drivers}.

On injected shifts in SECOM \cite{secom2008}, Diabetes130
\cite{clore2014diabetes130}, TEP \cite{chiang2001fault}, and TCGA
\cite{weinstein2013tcga}, mean share keeps a high selection AUC at five
corrupted centers out of the reported grid
(Table~\ref{tab:federated_real_data}).
At ten corrupted centers the ranking moves: SECOM mean-share AUC falls to
\(0.708\) and TEP to \(0.580\), while TCGA stays at \(0.977\).

agree on one fact and not on another.

a clean signal remains.

design.

A homogeneous synthetic check uses \(d=10\), \(n=2400\), and \(K=6\)
blocks, three shifted and three clean, with equal sizes or a
Dirichlet\((0.5)\) allocation.
The regression on the clean blocks is linear in the first five coordinates
plus standard normal noise.
Covariate shifts are a mean shift, a covariance shift, or a mixture shift.
Concept drifts added to the regression are linear, sine, cubic,
interaction, and threshold.
Across that list the Spearman correlation between the merged vector and
the pooled vector stays in \([0.79,0.93]\), averaged over two seeds and
both size allocations.
The highest entry is linear drift with sample-size weights (\(0.933\)).
The lowest is the uniform average under the product interaction
(\(0.803\)).
With no corruption, a mixed block of two covariate-shift clients, two
concept-drift clients, one mixed client, and one clean client gives
Spearman \(0.838\) (uniform), \(0.867\) (\(n\)-weighted), \(0.842\)
(median), and \(0.879\) (client-agreement Shapley) against the centralized
importance.
Taking the top three or top five clients and then applying sample-size
weights raises that to \(0.925\) and \(0.929\).
Screening clients before averaging is the same idea as the
\(\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}\) weight, computed here by agreement with the
central vector rather than by a local skill score.

\subsection{Real data: images, WhyShift, and TabReD}
\label{sec:real-fed}

FEMNIST \cite{leaf_federated_dataset} and WhyShift
\cite{WhyShift_Attribution} check ranking consistency when the reference
is the pooled vector on the same images or the same table.
For a character pair, a \(30\)-component PCA is fit on the pooled pair and
each batch is cut into \(K\in\{5,10,20,30\}\) blocks.
The three uploaded vectors are random-forest domain importance,
leave-one-coordinate RBF MMD, and the increase in pseudo-outcome risk
under conditional permutation.
As a direct prediction problem, a random forest classifies digits \(0\)--\(9\)
from \(30\) pooled PCA scores on \(200{,}000\) images.
Importance is the drop in accuracy when a component is permuted.
Twenty blocks of \(10{,}000\) have equal size, so the uniform average and
the sample-size average coincide.
Their Spearman correlation with the pooled importance is \(0.996\)
(Pearson \(0.996\)).
Inverse-variance weighting yields \(0.993\).
A single block, before merging, already correlates at \(0.975\), and the
first four components are \(2,3,6,9\) in both the pooled vector and the
merge.
Information loss from cutting the sample is small when every block is a
copy of the same prediction problem.

Pseudo-outcome risk on the same images does not follow this pattern.
With a balanced random treatment arm and the digit as the outcome, the
pooled PO-risk vector has mean absolute entry \(0.0007\).
Inverse-variance and sample-size merges correlate with it at \(-0.02\) and
\(-0.01\).
The treatment is independent of the image, so the wrapper has no residual
contrast.
The WhyShift folds in Table~\ref{tab:pol-ood} are the tabular version of
the same split: covariate wrappers aggregate, and PO-risk does not.

\subsection{Statistical justification}
\label{sec:theory}

Write \(V_{j,k}\) for the importance of feature \(j\) at site \(k\).
Dependence across features inside a site is unrestricted.
Across sites the uploads are treated as independent.
This is a statement about the uploaded vectors, not about multi-modal
attribution with several loaders.
The guarantee uses consistency of the local estimator in mean and
variance: \(\hat V_{j,k}\xrightarrow{p} V_{j,k}^{\star}\) and
\(\hat\sigma_{j,k}^2\xrightarrow{p}\sigma_{j,k}^2\).
Normalizing each upload onto the simplex gives
\(\|V_k\|_2\le\|V_k\|_1=1\), so the aggregate converges to its
expectation, and the Matrix Bernstein inequality upgrades pointwise
consistency to a uniform bound over the \(p\) coordinates.
The bound does not require the \(p\times p\) covariance of the
coordinates.
The dimension enters as \(\log(2p/\delta)\).

\begin{quote}
\noindent\textbf{Theorem (Matrix Bernstein bound for aggregated VIMP).}
Let \(V_k\in[0,1]^p\) with \(\sum_{j=1}^p V_{k,j}=1\) for each
\(k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), and let
\(\bar V=K^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^K V_k\).
For any \(\delta\in(0,1)\), with probability at least \(1-\delta\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:matrix_bernstein_main}
\bigl\| \bar V - \mathbb{E}[\bar V] \bigr\|_2
\le
\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2p/\delta)}{K}}
+
\frac{2\log(2p/\delta)}{3K}.
\end{equation}
\end{quote}

The same simplex bound is the starting point for a mean, a median, and
inverse-variance weights.
The median and the mean satisfy it once a consistent variance estimator
exists, for example from the bootstrap, the jackknife, or the
bag of little bootstraps.
Inverse-variance weights need the additional lower bound in
Assumption~(A6).
If that bound fails for some site, truncated weights or equal weights
restore the coordinate-wise inequality below.
Each site uploads its normalized vector once.
Relative to iterative synchronization of a high-dimensional model, the
communication is smaller by a factor of order \(1/K\), and no raw row is
sent.

\paragraph{Claim.}
\label{sec:fed-ood-vimp-tight}
Under Assumptions~(A1)--(A4), the one-shot aggregate obeys the scalar and
coordinate-wise bounds \eqref{eq:scalar}--\eqref{eq:linf}.
Under the gap condition~(A5), the empirical Top-\(K\) set of \(\bar V\)
equals that of \(\mathbb{E}\bar V\) on the event
\(\{\|\bar V-\mathbb{E}\bar V\|_{\infty}<\mathrm{margin}/2\}\).
Under~(A6), the leading range factor in \eqref{eq:linf} may be replaced
by an empirical variance proxy.
Any simplex upload satisfies the assumptions.
They do not require a low-rank or sparse model for
\(\Sigma=\mathrm{Cov}(V_k)\).
The inequalities are not asserted to dominate the spectral bound for every
\(\Sigma\), and they are not asserted to be minimax.
They are enough for coordinate-wise control and for a Top-\(K\) corollary.

\paragraph{Assumptions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(A1)] \(V_k\in[0,1]^p\) with \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}V_k=1\);
  the sites are independent;
  \(\bar V=\sum_k w_k V_k\) with \(w_k\ge 0\) and \(\sum_k w_k=1\).
\item[(A2)] \(\hat R:=\max_k\|V_k\|_2\) and \(R_{\star}\ge\hat R\)
  (for example \(R_{\star}=1\)).
\item[(A3)] \(K_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is the effective sample size induced by
  \((w_k)\). It equals \(K\) when \(w_k=1/K\).
\item[(A4)] A fixed contrast \(a\in\mathbb{R}^p\) with \(\|a\|_2\le 1\);
  \(v_a=\mathrm{Var}(a^{\top}V_1)\) under identical site laws, or the
  analogous weighted variance otherwise.
\item[(A5)] The population shares separate a designated index set of size
  \(K\) from its complement by a margin \(\mathrm{margin}>0\).
\item[(A6)] Consistent estimates
  \(\hat\sigma_k^2\ge\underline\sigma^2>0\) for the coordinates or the
  contrast in use.
\end{itemize}
Dependence among coordinates inside a site is unrestricted, and \(\Sigma\)
is not estimated.
No condition is imposed on \(\mathrm{rank}(\Sigma)\).
In particular,~(A1) and~(A2) hold for every FSDS share.

\paragraph{Spectral baseline.}
Under~(A1)--(A3) and a bound \(R\) on
\(\|w_k(V_k-\mathbb{E}V_k)\|_2\), Matrix Bernstein
\cite{tropp2012user,mackey2014matrix} yields, with probability at least
\(1-\delta\),
\[
\|\bar V-\mathbb{E}\bar V\|_2
\le
C\Bigl(
R\sqrt{\frac{\log(2p/\delta)}{K_{\mathrm{eff}}}}
+
R\frac{\log(2p/\delta)}{K_{\mathrm{eff}}}
\Bigr).
\]
The substitution used below is \(R\leftarrow R_{\star}\) from~(A2).

\paragraph{Scalar and \(\ell_\infty\) bounds.}
Under~(A1)--(A4), with probability at least \(1-\delta\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scalar}
\bigl|a^{\top}(\bar V-\mathbb{E}\bar V)\bigr|
\le
C\Bigl(
\sqrt{\frac{v_a\log(2/\delta)}{K_{\mathrm{eff}}}}
+
R_{\star}\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{K_{\mathrm{eff}}}
\Bigr),
\end{equation}
and, by a union bound over coordinates,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:linf}
\|\bar V-\mathbb{E}\bar V\|_{\infty}
\le
C\Bigl(
R_{\star}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2p/\delta)}{K_{\mathrm{eff}}}}
+
R_{\star}\frac{\log(2p/\delta)}{K_{\mathrm{eff}}}
\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:linf} is the deviation that enters the Top-\(K\) event.
It is not presented as a strict improvement of the spectral-norm
inequality for every covariance.

\paragraph{Top-\(K\) corollary.}
Under~(A1)--(A5), the event
\(\{\|\bar V-\mathbb{E}\bar V\|_{\infty}<\mathrm{margin}/2\}\) implies
equality of the empirical and population Top-\(K\) sets.
Together with~\eqref{eq:linf}, a sufficient condition is
\[
C\Bigl(
R_{\star}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2p/\delta)}{K_{\mathrm{eff}}}}
+
R_{\star}\frac{\log(2p/\delta)}{K_{\mathrm{eff}}}
\Bigr)
<
\frac12\,\mathrm{margin}.
\]
If the margin lies below this threshold, Top-\(K\) instability comes from
insufficient separation of the population shares, not from the choice
among the mean, the median, and inverse-variance weights.
Concordance of the full Spearman ranking is not claimed.

\paragraph{Empirical Bernstein.}
Under~(A1)--(A4) and~(A6), empirical Bernstein
\cite{wainwright2019high,vershynin2018high} replaces the leading factor
\(R_{\star}\) in~\eqref{eq:linf} by a consistent variance proxy, up to a
remainder of order \(R_{\star}\log(2p/\delta)/K_{\mathrm{eff}}\).
The same variance input is required for inverse-variance weights
\cite{dersim}.
If the lower bound in~(A6) fails at some site, truncated weights or equal
weights restore~\eqref{eq:linf}.

The bound concentrates \(\bar V\) around \(\mathbb{E}\bar V\).
It does not identify which sites belong in that expectation.
A noise site and a drifted site can both upload a large vector.
The expectation of the uniform average then mixes a concept with a
consensus breaker, and the inequality still holds around that mixture.
Section~\ref{sec:drivers} is the justification for which sites enter the
sum.

\subsection{Justifying shift drivers across sites}
\label{sec:drivers}

A driver is a site whose conditional distribution \(P(Y\mid X)\) has
changed, in a way a local model can learn, relative to a shared burn-in.
The ten-site design makes that set known.
Covariates are the ACS Income and ACS Public Coverage extracts used by
WhyShift, California 2018, winsorized and standardized on the analysis
sample, then cut at random into \(K=10\) sites of \(1600\) rows.
Every site therefore shares the same empirical \(P(X)\).
Each site is \(T=16\) batches of \(100\) rows.
The first four batches are the burn-in, still labeled by the real outcome
\(y^{\mathrm{base}}\).
At batch \(t^{\star}=8\), four clean sites keep \(y^{\mathrm{base}}\),
three linear sites rewrite an upper tail of a designated coordinate
\(X_1\) by a site-specific halfspace whose positive rate matches the
baseline, two nonlinear sites rewrite every activated row by a product
rule or by \(\sin(X_1)+X_3^2\), again rate-matched, and one noise site
draws independent \(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) labels.
The noise site destroys consensus.
It is not a driver.
There is no new concept to learn.

The site score uses a random forest with \(25\) trees, depth \(8\), and
minimum leaf size \(5\).
Local probabilities on the most recent six batches are three-fold
cross-fit.
Reference probabilities are the average of two forests, each trained on a
subset of the pooled burn-in whose size equals the local training set.
On a region \(\mathcal{R}\) with both classes present, at least fifty
current rows, and at least eighty reference rows,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:delta-region-fed}
\Delta_k(\mathcal{R})
=
\bigl(B(\mu_0^{\mathrm{sm}})-B(\mu_k^{\mathrm{oof}})\bigr)_+
\cdot
\bigl(2(\mathrm{AUC}(\mu_k^{\mathrm{oof}})-1/2)\bigr)_+,
\end{equation}
and the reported score is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:delta-cf-fed}
\Delta_k^{\mathrm{cf}}
=
\max_{\mathcal{R}\in\{\mathrm{all},\,\mathrm{high},\,\mathrm{low}\}}
\Delta_k(\mathcal{R}).
\end{equation}
High and low are the two sides of the burn-in median of \(X_1\).
The first factor is the excess Brier risk of the size-matched reference.
The second factor is zero when the local model does not beat a coin.
The concept contrast on the winning region is
\(\tau_k^2=\mathrm{mean}(\mu_k^{\mathrm{oof}}-\mu_0^{\mathrm{sm}})^2\),
and \(\tau_k^{2,\mathrm{g}}\) multiplies it by the same skill factor.
Pairwise concordance \(C(s;\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\) is the fraction of
pairs with a strict inequality \(s_a>s_b\).
The target is \(C(\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}};\mathcal{P},\mathcal{C})>0.8\),
where \(\mathcal{P}\) is the five structured sites and \(\mathcal{C}\) is
the four clean sites.

At full rewrite, abrupt activation, and a median tail, over four seeds,
that concordance equals \(1\) on both tasks, in every seed, for linear
sites alone, for nonlinear sites alone, and for \(\mathcal{P}\) against
the noise site.
On Income the linear score is \(0.253\) on the upper tail and \(0.024\) on
the whole window, because hours worked pile up at \(40\) and the median
tail is about \(23\%\) of the rows.
The noise site has skill \(0.008\) and \(\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}=0.001\),
while its ungated contrast is \(\tau^2=0.096\), larger than the clean
contrast \(0.015\).
The skill factor is what refuses to call that gap a driver.
Pooled excess, without size matching and without the skill factor, ranks
the Income noise site above every linear site: its concordance against
the noise site is \(0.400\), which is exactly the two nonlinear sites
beating the noise site and the three linear sites failing to.
Label total variation and the covariate arms (domain AUC, RBF MMD) do not
separate \(\mathcal{P}\) from \(\mathcal{C}\).
The joint classifier on \((X,Y)\) clears \(0.8\) against clean sites on
average and ranks the noise site as the stronger departure.

A Pareto rank on \((\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}},\tau^{2,\mathrm{g}},\text{time jump})\)
has the same concordance of \(1\).
Mean ranks on Income are \(1.875\) (nonlinear), \(3.250\) (linear),
\(6.625\) (clean), and \(6.750\) (noise).
The undominated front is mostly the nonlinear sites.
Linear sites are often dominated by a nonlinear site, because the
nonlinear rule rewrites every row, and they remain strictly ahead of every
clean site and of the noise site.
The time curve of \(\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}\), on windows of four batches,
leaves zero after \(t^{\star}\) and levels off at batch \(11\), the first
endpoint whose window lies entirely inside the drift.
An online threshold at the maximum of the first two endpoints plus \(0.01\)
hits every nonlinear site and, on Public Coverage, every linear site.
Income linear sites hit in \(0.833\) of replicates.
Income clean sites cross the threshold with rate \(0.375\); those crossings
have mean absolute error \(4.17\) batches, so the typical alarm is not a
call at \(t^{\star}\).
The derivative of the curve marks the start of the plateau, about one
window later.

Concept-group logistic scores on \((Z_1,Z_2)\), on \(Z_1 Z_2\), and on
\((\sin Z_1, Z_3^2)\) name the mechanism only on the region where it was
planted.
Top-five precision of \(\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}\) is \(1\) in every seed.
On the full window, product and sine-square recall are \(1\) and linear
recall is \(0.500\).
On the upper tail, linear recall is \(0.917\) and product recall falls to
\(0.375\).
Shift-permutation importance on a drifted site recovers the planted
coordinates (AUC at least \(0.906\) on linear sites).
Weighting those uploads by \(\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}\) raises Income recovery
AUC from \(0.869\) to \(0.917\).
Leave-one-site-out influence on the uniform shift vector is about
\(0.01\)--\(0.03\) and is not larger for the drivers than for the clean
sites.
The same negative result is Table~\ref{tab:tabred-fsds-loso}.
The object that separates drivers is \(\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}\), computed
from the site's own labels, before the aggregate is formed.

The \(0.8\) target is not uniform in the design.
On Income, it holds for rewrite probabilities \(0.7\) and \(1\) down to a
linear tail of about \(17\%\) of rows, abrupt or gradual.
It falls below \(0.8\) at rewrite probability \(0.4\) (mean concordance
\(0.783\), worst seed \(0.650\), on the main tail), and at a tail of about
\(8\%\) combined with rewrite probability \(0.7\) and a four-batch ramp
(\(0.683\), worst seed \(0.500\)).
On that weak main-tail slice the whole-window score, without the region
maximum, has concordance \(0.433\).

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:fed-summary}

Each site uploads one simplex vector, once.
The four answers below are the operating rule for that upload.

\paragraph{When and how to aggregate.}
The default is the uniform weight \(w_k=1/K\), used when the sites are
noisy copies of one shared shift and are about the same size.
On TabReD the largest site fraction is \(0.24\), and
\(\Delta(n|S|-\mathrm{unif})\) is about \(0.01\)
(mean Spearman \(0.453\) uniform, \(0.466\) for \(n|S|\)).
The weight changes in four situations, and only in those
(Table~\ref{tab:weight-rule}).
When size mismatch is high, set \(w_k\propto n_k\) or use the
size-and-support weight \(n|S|\): Spearman with the mixed pool rises from
\(0.620\) to \(0.905\), a gain of \(+0.285\), and the gain grows from
\(+0.24\) at heterogeneity \(0\) to \(+0.37\) at heterogeneity \(1\).
Rank averaging moves the other way, from \(0.658\) to \(0.580\).
When one or two uploaded vectors are replaced, use the coordinate-wise
median or client-agreement Shapley.
Two random replacements leave the uniform Spearman at \(0.055\) and the
sample-size weight at \(0.156\); the median stays at \(0.741\) and
client-agreement Shapley at \(0.737\).
Client-agreement Shapley is a weight on sites.
When a consistent variance estimate is bounded below and the wrapper is a
covariate score, use inverse-variance weights.
On ten WhyShift folds the random-forest domain classifier moves from
\(0.848\) on one fold to \(0.887\) with sample-size weights and to
\(0.918\) with inverse-variance weights; MMD-LOCO moves from \(0.891\) to
\(0.985\).
When the wrapper is a concept score, set
\(w_k\propto\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}_k\).
Sample-size weights on WhyShift pseudo-outcome risk lower the Spearman
from \(0.410\) to \(0.185\); weighting the Income shift uploads by
\(\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}\) raises recovery AUC from \(0.869\) to \(0.917\).
When every weight returns the same Spearman and that Spearman misses the
whole batch, stop retuning.
Cooking-time \texttt{num\_105} has pairwise concordance \(0.884\),
site-to-whole correlation \(-0.198\), and aggregator headroom about zero.
The split is the defect.

\paragraph{How robust the aggregate is.}
Selection and rank correlation break at different places on the product
grid \(n_{\mathrm{bad}}\times\mathrm{SNR}\times\alpha\)
(Table~\ref{tab:Product_HighDimResults}).
At full local support and SNR \(=0.5\), precision at \(K\) of the mean,
the median, and inverse-variance weights is still \(1\) when all twelve
sites are corrupted, while Spearman of the mean has fallen to \(0.719\).
At SNR \(=0\) and twelve corrupted sites the selection AUC of the mean is
\(0.479\) and its precision is \(0\).
One remaining clean site is enough for the mean: at
\(n_{\mathrm{bad}}=11\), SNR \(=0.05\), and \(\alpha=1\), precision of the
mean is \(0.92\) and precision of the median is \(0.14\).
Thin support weakens the median before any site is corrupted.
At \(\alpha=0.25\), SNR \(=0.5\), and \(n_{\mathrm{bad}}=0\), median AUC
is \(0.768\) and median precision is \(0.29\), against AUC \(1\) and
precision \(1\) for the mean and for inverse-variance weights.
A corrupted site that reports a small variance takes excess
inverse-variance mass: one bad site, variance ratio \(0.44\), receives
weight \(0.17\), and its Spearman is \(0.844\) against \(0.959\) for the
uniform mean, with precision still \(1\).
Assumption~(A6) floors that variance.
Under a Dirichlet allocation of site sizes, uniform weights and the
median keep Spearman \(1\) with the clean covariate-shift aggregate down
to concentration \(0.05\).
Inverse-variance Spearman on the same grid is lower, \(0.610\) for concept
drift at concentration \(10\).
Five covariate-shift sites pooled with five concept-drift sites correlate
about \(0.30\) with the clean aggregate, for every aggregator that is
defined.
On SECOM, Diabetes130, TEP, and TCGA, five corrupted centers leave the
selected set largely intact.
At ten corrupted centers, mean-share AUC is \(0.708\) on SECOM, \(0.580\)
on TEP, and \(0.977\) on TCGA.
On equal FEMNIST blocks the predictive permutation ranking correlates
\(0.996\) with the pooled vector (inverse-variance \(0.993\); one block
already \(0.975\)).
Pseudo-outcome risk on a treatment independent of the image correlates at
about zero.
A homogeneous synthetic list of covariate shifts and concept drifts keeps
merged-versus-pooled Spearman in \([0.79,0.93]\).
Leave-one-site-out deletion moves the aggregate by about \(0.05\) whether
the aggregate is aligned or not, so influence does not diagnose a shared
wrong direction.

\paragraph{How a primary driver is found.}
A driver is named before the aggregate is formed.
The site score \(\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}\) is the size-matched excess Brier
risk of a cross-fit local forest over a burn-in reference, multiplied by
local skill \(\max\bigl(2(\mathrm{AUC}-1/2),0\bigr)\), then maximized over
the whole window and the two sides of the burn-in median of \(X_1\)
\eqref{eq:delta-region-fed}--\eqref{eq:delta-cf-fed}.
On the ten-site design every site shares the ACS empirical \(P(X)\).
Four sites stay clean, three carry a linear concept on an upper tail, two
rewrite every activated row by a product or by \(\sin(X_1)+X_3^2\), and
one draws \(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) labels.
At full rewrite, abrupt activation, and a median tail, over four seeds,
pairwise concordance of the five structured sites over the four clean
sites equals \(1\) on Income and on Public Coverage, in every seed, for
linear sites alone, for nonlinear sites alone, and against the noise site.
The Income noise site has skill \(0.008\) and
\(\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}=0.001\), while its ungated contrast is
\(\tau^2=0.096\) against a clean contrast of \(0.015\).
The skill factor is what keeps that gap out of the driver set.
Pooled excess on Income has concordance \(0.400\) against the noise site.
Mean Pareto ranks on Income are \(1.875\) (nonlinear), \(3.250\) (linear),
\(6.625\) (clean), and \(6.750\) (noise).
The same score, used as a site weight, raises Income shift-recovery AUC
from \(0.869\) to \(0.917\).
Leave-one-site-out influence on the uniform shift vector is about
\(0.01\)--\(0.03\) and is not larger on the drivers.
The \(0.8\) concordance target holds at rewrite probabilities \(0.7\) and
\(1\) down to a linear tail of about \(17\%\) of rows.
It is \(0.783\) (worst seed \(0.650\)) at rewrite probability \(0.4\) on
the main tail, and \(0.683\) (worst seed \(0.500\)) on an \(8\%\) tail with
rewrite probability \(0.7\) and a four-batch ramp.
On that weak main-tail slice the whole-window score has concordance
\(0.433\).

\paragraph{Theoretical support.}
Normalize each upload so that \(V_k\in[0,1]^p\) and
\(\sum_j V_{k,j}=1\).
Then \(\|V_k\|_2\le 1\).
For the uniform aggregate, with probability at least \(1-\delta\),
\[
\bigl\|\bar V-\mathbb{E}\bar V\bigr\|_2
\le
\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2p/\delta)}{K}}
+\frac{2\log(2p/\delta)}{3K},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:matrix_bernstein_main}.
Assumptions~(A1)--(A4) extend the same Bernstein bound to a weighted
aggregate through the effective sample size \(K_{\mathrm{eff}}\) and a
range \(R_{\star}\ge\max_k\|V_k\|_2\).
The scalar bound \eqref{eq:scalar} controls any fixed contrast.
The coordinate-wise bound \eqref{eq:linf} controls every coordinate at
once, with dimension entering only as \(\log(2p/\delta)\).
Under the margin condition~(A5),
\(\|\bar V-\mathbb{E}\bar V\|_{\infty}<\mathrm{margin}/2\) implies that
the empirical Top-\(K\) set equals the population Top-\(K\) set.
A margin below that deviation means the population shares are too close
for the Top-\(K\) set to be stable.
Full Spearman concordance is not part of the claim.
Inverse-variance weights need the variance floor in~(A6).
If the floor fails, truncated weights or equal weights restore
\eqref{eq:linf}.
Sites are independent across \(k\); dependence among coordinates inside a
site is unrestricted, and the \(p\times p\) covariance is not estimated.
Communication is one upload of \(p\) floats per site, \(O(Kp)\) in total.
The bound concentrates \(\bar V\) around \(\mathbb{E}\bar V\).
Which sites enter that expectation is the driver score above.

The construction attributes feature-level shift from one upload per site.
Open directions are streaming updates, modality-wise pseudo-outcome
contributions, and a differentially private upload that preserves the same
concentration.
