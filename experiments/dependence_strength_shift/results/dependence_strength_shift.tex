\section*{Dependence strength shift versus PO-risk ranking}

For each feature $X_j$ and batch $t\in\{0,1\}$, fit
$m_{t,j}(X_{-j})=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_t[X_j\mid X_{-j}]$ and score the held-out correlation ratio
\begin{equation}
\eta^{2}_{t,j}=1-\frac{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[(X_j-m_{t,j})^2\bigr]}{\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(X_j)},
\qquad A_j=\eta^{2}_{1,j}-\eta^{2}_{0,j}.
\end{equation}
Signed $A_j$ is the dependence-strength shift: positive means $X_{-j}$ explains more of $X_j$ on the target batch.

The same fits give a PO-risk ranking. With propensity $\pi$ and contrast $\tau=m_{1,j}-m_{0,j}$,
\begin{equation}
R(\tau)=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[(\phi-\tau)^2\bigr],
\qquad \Delta^{\mathrm{PO}}_j=R(0)-R(\tau),
\end{equation}
where $\phi$ is the doubly robust pseudo-outcome for treating batch as the ``treatment''.
Ranking features by $\Delta^{\mathrm{PO}}_j$ (equivalently by the PO-risk gap) answers whether the
\emph{conditional mean relationship} changed, not whether dependence strengthened or weakened.

\subsection*{Reading}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Spearman$(A)$ asks whether the method recovers the signed order of dependence intensity.
\item The PO-risk ranking $\Delta^{\mathrm{PO}}$ is the natural companion for \emph{relationship} change;
it is not a strength score, and it systematically over-ranks flips.
\item Use both: PO-risk to flag that $m_{1,j}-m_{0,j}$ moved; $A_j$ to say whether redundancy rose or fell.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Quant factor residuals}

In a multi-factor panel write $F_j=\beta_j^\top G+\varepsilon_j$. Pairwise residual correlation
$\mathrm{Corr}(\varepsilon_j,\varepsilon_k)$ is the linear special case. Applying the same
estimators to the residual matrix $(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_p)$ across two windows yields
\begin{equation}
A_j(\varepsilon)=\eta^{2}_{1,j}(\varepsilon)-\eta^{2}_{0,j}(\varepsilon),
\qquad
\Delta^{\mathrm{PO}}_j(\varepsilon)=R_j(0)-R_j(\tau),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ whether residual $j$ became more/less reconstructible from the other residuals, versus whether
the residual conditional map flipped. The incremental matrix
$V_{j\leftarrow k}=\mathrm{R}^2(\varepsilon_j\mid\varepsilon_{-j})-\mathrm{R}^2(\varepsilon_j\mid\varepsilon_{-\{j,k\}})$
attributes the redundancy change to a particular residual partner---the residual analogue of
``which other factor's idiosyncratic piece started explaining mine.''
See \texttt{factor\_residuals.py} and \texttt{run\_factor\_demo}.
