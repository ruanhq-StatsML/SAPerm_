\paragraph{Claim 1: adding meta-features raises power --- where it holds.}
The claim holds for CFPerm on gender, where concatenating $M$ raises power by $+.06$ (sentiment, $.92\to.98$)
and $+.08$ (syntax, $.60\to.68$; Table~\ref{tab:gender_power}), and for RFPerm on gender syntax
($.27\to.36$, $+.09$, $B{=}150$).
On Amazon, the RFPerm gains come from the spaCy arm $S$, which does not encode the window-selection
variables: sentiment $+.12$ (MiniLM, $.82\to.94$) and $+.05$ (Qwen, $.89\to.93$); time $+.08$
($.16\to.24$) and $+.04$ ($.23\to.27$) (Table~\ref{tab:amazon_rf_b150}).
Only the MiniLM sentiment gain clearly exceeds the cell standard error ($\le .04$) and survives the
ordered split.
The claim does not hold for every test or arm, and we do not state it as such.

\paragraph{Why $M$ lowers RFPerm, DRPerm and RRPerm on meta-selected windows: absorption.}
The sentiment and syntax windows are drawn from the tails of two coordinates of $M$
(VADER compound, verb/noun ratio).
On Amazon, concatenating $M$ lowers RFPerm power there, by $-.18$/$-.23$ (sentiment, MiniLM/Qwen)
and $-.17$/$-.12$ (syntax).
All three tests ask whether $Y\mid X$ changes between windows.
Once the selection variable is in $X$, the window label is nearly a function of $X$:
$M$ predicts $W$ with CV AUC $.99$ (sentiment) and $1.00$ (syntax), and $R^2_W=.82$/$.86$.
The DR residual variance $\mathrm{Var}(W-\hat e)$ drops to $.24$/$.21$ of its embedding-only value
(Table~\ref{tab:amazon_absorption}).
The shift is then explained by the covariates, the conditional null is close to true, and the tests
correctly lose power.
The same mechanism produces the gender DRPerm drops ($.88\to.08$, $.60\to.12$) and RRPerm drops
($1.00\to.90$, $.74\to.32$).
This is absorption, not evidence that $M$ is uninformative:
$M$ is the most $y$-predictive block ($y$ CV AUC $.92$ vs.\ $.87$ for $Z$).
Absorption is also not a positivity failure in the strict sense: at most $1.8\%$ of the
cross-fitted $\hat e(X)$ fall below $.02$ or above $.98$.
Nor is it caused by the $M$--$y$ correlation alone: that correlation is equally strong in the time and
domain scenarios, where adding $M$ leaves $\mathrm{Var}(W-\hat e)$ unchanged (ratio $.98$, $1.12$).

\paragraph{The spaCy arm and its limits.}
$S$ (36 spaCy features, with no VADER, sentiment lexicon or verb/noun ratio) avoids absorption on the
sentiment window: it predicts $W$ with AUC $.62$, and the residual-variance ratio is $.98$.
That is why RFPerm gains there.
On the syntax window, RFPerm still drops ($-.15$ MiniLM, $-.09$ Qwen), because the POS ratios in $S$
are correlated with the verb/noun selection variable (AUC$_W=.83$, ratio $.80$): absorption is partial.
On the cross-domain shift with Qwen, both concatenations lower power
($.69\to.37$ with $M$, $\to.43$ with $S$), while with MiniLM both change it by only $+.03$.
Qwen's 24 PCs already separate the domains, and $M$ or $S$ alone barely does (AUC$_W\approx.67$).
The most likely explanation is dilution: with $m_{\mathrm{try}}=\sqrt p$, adding 32--36 weakly
informative columns lowers the share of splits drawn on the embedding coordinates.
We do not claim that $S$ is more informative than $M$: $S$ predicts $y$ less well
(CV AUC $.84$ vs.\ $.92$).
Its value is that it complements the embedding without directly encoding the window-selection variable.

\paragraph{Meta only.}
$M$ alone has the lowest RFPerm power in every Amazon shift ($.11$--$.49$).
This is expected: the shifts live mostly in the embedding, and $M$ is a 32-dimensional summary that
cannot replace the backbone.

\paragraph{Claim 2: higher-order interactions.}
The claim that higher-order interactions matter only when each modality is extracted separately
is not covered by this experiment.
Amazon is text-only, so there is a single modality and no cross-modal interaction to test.
Any support for claim~2 has to come from the existing multimodal results.

\paragraph{Test details and caveats.}
RFPerm uses the official \texttt{RFPerm::permrf} direction: one-sided upper-tail
$p=(1+\#\{d_b\ge d_0\})/(B+1)$ with $d_0=\overline{\mathrm{loss}}_{\mathrm{new}}-\overline{\mathrm{loss}}_{\mathrm{val}}$,
so the test rejects when the new window is harder to predict.
The two-sided $p$ gives the same sign for every difference of at least $.05$.
The Python permutation step was checked against \texttt{RFPerm:::permTest\_} (identical $d_0$; upper-tail
$p$ within $1/(B+1)$). The end-to-end R backend was not rerun at $B{=}150$.
With a chronological (ordered) train/validation split, the drop from $M$ on meta-selected windows is
stable ($-.05$ to $-.35$), and the MiniLM spaCy gains shrink but stay positive
(sentiment $+.07$, time $+.03$).
The smaller Qwen spaCy gains do not survive the ordered split (sentiment $-.02$, time $-.01$), and the
MiniLM domain changes turn negative ($-.15$ with $M$, $-.07$ with $S$).
We therefore report the spaCy gains as robust for MiniLM sentiment only.
Null rejection rates stay at or below $.09$ ($B{=}150$).
The embedding dimension is fixed at $k{=}24$ PCs. A kneedle elbow on the effective-rank curve sits much
higher ($k\approx174$ gender/bias, $\approx164$ Amazon Gift).
The elbow describes the variance structure of $Z$ only; it does not guarantee optimal power.
More PCs carry more signal but also more noise coordinates for the RF, so the choice of $k$ is an
information/noise trade-off that we did not tune for power.
All Amazon artifacts are keyed by row id and text SHA-1 of a single frame per spec, and alignment
is re-asserted at load time.
