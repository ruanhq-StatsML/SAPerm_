\begin{abstract}
The product answers one query.
Ten days ago the IP-block feature of cohort~A spiked.
If that day is put back on its reference path, what is today's purchase distribution for cohort~B, at which lag does the discrepancy peak, and which block carries it?
The shipped object is a conditional-distribution counterfactual \citep{chernozhukov2013inference}, scored by random-Fourier MMD\textsuperscript{2} \citep{gretton2012kernel,rahimi2007random} under a conditional DDPM \citep{ho2020denoising}.
On a ten-site linear panel with a known kernel, the validation path peaks at day~10 on the weekly site, the intervened block is the IP feature, and the isolated copy of the same site stays on the floor.
The ratio of that score to the no-intervention baseline is not a stable effect size: on a shared observational bandwidth it still moves from $8.9$ to $46.4$.
The same kernel, inverted to a squared location shift, stays at $0.80$ (coefficient of variation $0.01$).
\end{abstract}

\section{The query}
\label{sec:query}

Cohorts are sites.
On a shared calendar, site $s$ has an IP-block series $B_{s,t}\in\mathbb{R}$ and a purchase index $Y_{s,t}$.
The business query fixes a spike day $t_0$ and a horizon $h=10$:
\begin{quote}
If $B_{s,t_0}$ had followed the reference path, what would the conditional law of $Y_{s,t_0+10}$ be?
\end{quote}
The product returns five objects for that query:
a point estimate of the distributional discrepancy and a bootstrap interval;
the path $\{\Delta_h\}$ over horizons;
the scale weights of the conditioner;
the fraction of counterfactual states inside the reference support;
and the five-layer falsification report of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}.

\section{What the product claims}
\label{sec:contract}

The intervention replaces the lagged IP block with the reference draw and keeps covariates at their observed values.
The target is the conditional law
\[
P_\theta\bigl(Y_{s,t}\mid B^{\mathrm{cf}}_{s,t-1:t-K},X_{s,t}\bigr),
\]
the ceteris paribus conditional counterfactual of \citet{chernozhukov2013inference}.
A structural effect along a named channel is a different target \citep{pearl2009causality}.
Risk control, recommendation, and social transmission are represented by one site-specific distributed lag.
They are not separately identified, and the product does not label a channel.
MMD has no physical unit.
The number that may be shown next to a decision is the raw random-Fourier score together with its interval; the ratio to the no-intervention baseline is a calibration diagnostic.
A conditioning vector outside the reference support does not enter the headline.

\section{Score}
\label{sec:score}

Let $P_{\mathrm{obs}}$ be the frozen sample of purchases on the query day, and let $P_\theta(\,\cdot\mid\mathrm{cf})$ be the DDPM sample under the reference history.
With $D=100$ random Fourier features of the Gaussian kernel \citep{rahimi2007random,gretton2012kernel},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:delta}
\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}
=
\mathrm{MMD}^{2}_{\mathrm{RFF}}
\bigl(
P_{\mathrm{obs}}(Y_{s,t_0+h}),\,
P_\theta(Y_{s,t_0+h}\mid\mathrm{cf})
\bigr).
\end{equation}
The no-intervention baseline $\Delta^{\mathrm{base}}$ uses the same sampler on the observed history.
The normalized diagnostic is $\widetilde\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}=\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}/\max(\Delta^{\mathrm{base}},10^{-3})$.
The interval is the percentile interval of~\eqref{eq:delta} under $400$ resamples of the two finite samples, with the Fourier features held fixed.
Bandwidth is the median pairwise distance, unless a sensitivity setting scales it.

\section{Landing panel}
\label{sec:panel}

The landing test is a ten-site linear conditional distribution.
Forty-eight windows, forty days each, spike day $t_0=18$, additive spike of size $\mathrm{dose}\times 1$.
The last twelve windows are the validation slice.
The conditional mean is linear in a fourteen-day lag, a social term at lag~$2$, and three covariates:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:mu}
\mu_{s,t}
&=
\alpha_s
+\sum_{k=1}^{14}\omega_s(k)\,B_{s,t-k}
+\beta^{\mathrm{soc}}_s\cdot\mathrm{overlap}_{s}\cdot B_{s,t-2} \nonumber\\
&\quad
+\gamma^\top X_{s,t}
+0.15\,U_s .
\end{align}
The conditional standard deviation is $0.48\,\sigma_s\bigl(1+0.15\,|\mathrm{signal}_{s,t}|\bigr)$.
$U_s$ is unobserved.
A bump kernel has its peak at a site-specific lag; a decay kernel puts its mass on recent lags.
Isolation multiplies $\omega_s$ by zero after the kernel is recorded, so the IP spike is still seen.

At unit dose the population mean shift of site~0 at lag~10 is $0.95$.
Doses on the validation windows cycle through $\{0.40,0.70,1.00,1.30\}$, so the average oracle shift on those windows is $0.807$.
Site~6 is the only social transmitter, and its overlap grid is $\{0.20,0.45,0.70,0.92\}$ at a fixed dose of~$1$.
Site~0 also has a varying overlap, and $\beta^{\mathrm{soc}}_0=0$, so overlap is a negative control there.

The same conditioner shape accepts a live retail panel.
A store is a site, a promo or a competitor shock is $B_t$, and the daily sales distribution is $Y_t$ \citep{makridakis2022m5}.
The landing test keeps the kernel known so that a wrong peak can be seen.
A live panel does not have that oracle; the five layers below are the acceptance test that remains.

\section{Model that ships}
\label{sec:model}

The landing call is the package. Nothing else is required to reproduce the card.

\begin{verbatim}
import ddpm_cf_attribution
result = ddpm_cf_attribution.run()
\end{verbatim}

\noindent
\texttt{result["primary\_driver"]} is the site, the lag, and the block.
\texttt{result["headline"]} is $\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}$, the interval, and the path shape.
The call writes the five-layer report next to the package.

The diffused variable is the scalar purchase index.
A length-one series has no spatial U-Net, so the denoiser is a two-block MLP with adaptive layer normalisation, the scalar analogue of the adaptive group normalisation used by \citet{dhariwal2021diffusion}.
History reaches the denoiser only after a three-way softmax gate over lags $1$--$2$ (hourly), $3$--$5$ (daily), and $6$--$14$ (weekly).
The training loss is the noise-prediction loss of \citet{ho2020denoising}, with the lag block dropped on ten percent of steps.
The noise schedule is the cosine schedule of \citet{nichol2021improved}, sixty steps on the shipped retry.
Site identity enters as an embedding.
The isolation flag does not.
The acceptance weight for a scale is the normalised change in the predictive mean when that lag bucket is reset to its training mean.
The softmax is logged and is not the acceptance statistic, because a gate and a downstream weight are not separately identified.
On site~0 the softmax was $(0.066,0.045,0.889)$ and the ablation weight was $(0.17,0.15,0.68)$.
Both put the mass on the weekly bucket.

Before diffusion, a quantile-regression MVP \citep{koenker2005quantile} must clear a dose-response concordance of $0.70$.
On the validation windows the gradient-boosting quantiles scored $0.83$, and the run entered Stage~2.
The first diffusion fit failed the Wasserstein clause of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}.
One pre-registered retry, one hundred and twenty epochs at width $192$, is the fit reported below.
Its best validation noise loss was $0.397$.

The counterfactual path replaces $B_{s,t_0}$ with the pre-spike latent and leaves later innovations as observed.
In this panel the block has no autoregressive feedback, so that replacement is the rollout.
Each future day is then scored by~\eqref{eq:delta}.

\section{What the validation windows showed}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Headline window.}
One validation window with dose~$1$, site~0, horizon~$10$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:headline}
\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}=0.674,
\qquad
95\%\ \mathrm{CI}\ [0.442,\,0.906],
\qquad
\Delta^{\mathrm{base}}=0.021,
\qquad
\widetilde\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}=31.9 .
\end{equation}
The counterfactual mean sits $1.009$ below the observed mean.
The bandwidth-stable reading of the same kernel is the inverted squared shift.
On the twelve validation windows it is $0.808$, $0.798$, and $0.787$ at half, unit, and double bandwidth (coefficient of variation $0.01$), against the oracle mean of squared shifts $0.754$.
The reference-history support rate on the whole validation grid is $1.00$ at a coordinate $z$-score of~$4$.
A stress path that pushes the lag-10 distractor $b_2$ out by $4.5$ standard deviations is localised by a leave-one-coordinate nearest-neighbour score on that coordinate; clamping it cuts the neighbour distance from $4.05$ to $2.63$.
The real attribution path did not need that clamp.

\paragraph{Who moves today's purchases.}
Table~\ref{tab:path} averages the twelve validation windows.
The primary site is site~0.
Its path rises and then falls, with the peak on day~$10$.
Sites whose kernels sit on other lags peak on those lags and are flat at lag~$10$.
The isolated copy of the weekly primary, site~7, has mean $\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}=0.019$ against $0.400$ for site~0.

The top three sites by raw $\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}$ are $0$, $4$, and $1$, the same three sites as the oracle.

\paragraph{Which block.}
Replacing the IP lag gives $\Delta=0.400$.
Resetting the three lag-10 distractors to their training means gives $0.037$.
Resetting the covariates gives $0.099$.
The block that carries the query is the IP feature.

\paragraph{Where the past information sits.}
Placebo horizons $3$, $5$, $15$, and $20$ score $0.054$, $0.068$, $0.043$, and $0.067$, against $0.400$ at the true lag and $0.065$ for the no-intervention baseline.
The precision scan over days $1$ through $15$ peaks at day~$10$.
Daily site~2 peaks at day~$3$, immediate site~3 and the decay site peak at day~$1$, and the overlap site peaks at day~$2$, which is the only lag in its structural term.

\label{sec:protocol}

Thresholds were fixed before the run.
A layer fails closed.

\paragraph{Relative rank.}
Dose concordance on site~0 is $0.81$ and the Spearman correlation with dose is $0.69$, short of the $0.70$ clause, so that construction fails on its own.
The decay site ranks horizons with Spearman $-0.77$.
The same statistic on the weekly site is $+0.69$: a monotone-lag story is the wrong assumption for a day-10 kernel, and that failure is charged to the construction.
Overlap concordance on the social site is $0.87$; on the risk site the overlap Spearman is $-0.11$.
Isolation of the matched weekly site has paired Cohen's $d=-1.45$.
Three constructions pass.

\paragraph{Observational calibration.}
The probability integral transform \citep{dawid1984present} has Kolmogorov--Smirnov $p=0.445$.
The Hilbert--Schmidt dependence ratio \citep{gretton2005measuring} between generated outcomes and the conditioner, over the same ratio for observed outcomes, is $1.009$.
Mean per-cell Wasserstein distance is $0.245$ standard deviations of $Y$, above the clause of $0.10$.
A Gaussian oracle that sees only the features, putting the realised $U$ into the variance, sits at $0.166$ on the same comparison.
Two samples from one conditional sit at $0.071$.
The $0.10$ clause lies under the feature-only floor of this panel.
The DDPM is $0.079$ above that floor.
PIT and HSIC pass.
The layer still fails closed.

\paragraph{Proxies.}
An R-learner \citep{nie2021quasi} with the lag-10 IP feature as the continuous treatment estimates a site-0 coefficient of $+1.005$.
The rollout's mean gap has the same sign.
The mask gap $\Delta^{\mathrm{cf}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{mask}}=0.363$.
The excess of the purchase index over the spike day peaks on day~$10$, the same day as $\Delta_h$.
Four proxies agree.

\paragraph{Fragility.}
Four reference replacements of the spike day, residual, mean, resample, and zero, give raw scores $0.400$, $0.386$, $0.505$, and $0.387$ (coefficient of variation $0.14$).
The DDPM, the site-interacted linear Gaussian, and the quantile MVP score $0.400$, $0.386$, and $0.304$ (coefficient of variation $0.14$).
The E-value \citep{vanderweele2017sensitivity} at Cohen's $d=0.92$ is $4.06$.
One observational bandwidth, shared by the counterfactual and the baseline, still moves the raw ratio through $8.9$, $17.6$, and $46.4$ at multipliers $1/2$, $1$, and $2$ (coefficient of variation $0.81$).
That is the population shape of an RBF score divided by its null floor, and the frozen clause still fails on it.
Inverting that same kernel to a squared location shift gives $0.808$, $0.798$, and $0.787$ (coefficient of variation $0.01$).
The inversion does not rewrite the clause.
The stable objects are the ranking, the peak day, and this squared shift.

\section{Label on the decision}
\label{sec:label}

Ship the identity of the driver, not a unitless multiple.
In this landing the driver is site~0, lag~$10$, IP block $B$, weekly scale.
The purchase distribution moves with that cell: the dose-$1$ window scores $0.674$ with interval $[0.442,0.906]$, and the mean gap is about one purchase-index unit.
The path is a rise into day~$10$ and a decline after it.
Sites with the same spike and a closed kernel, including the isolated twin, do not move.

Put three labels on the card.
The normalized ratio is a calibration check; at the default bandwidth it is about $32$, and across the three protocol bandwidths it runs from $8.9$ to $46.4$.
The number to quote for the size of the move is the squared shift, $0.80$ on the validation windows against an oracle mean of squares $0.754$.
The Wasserstein clause of the observational layer fails against a threshold that sits below the unobserved-confounder floor ($0.166$); PIT and the dependence ratio pass, and the conditional mean gap ($0.794$ against an oracle of $0.807$) is the calibration fact the card should quote.
The E-value is $4.06$.
A randomised confirmation is still the next measurement if the decision isolates a live IP range.
The product does not name a risk, recommendation, or social channel.
