\section{Setup}
We ask whether spaCy+VADER meta-features $M\in\mathbb{R}^{37}$ give
\emph{incremental value} on top of BiasFinder BERT embeddings
$Z\in\mathbb{R}^{768}$ (PCA to $k{=}24$) for distribution-shift tests.
Feature sets:
\[
X_{\mathrm{embed}}=Z_{\mathrm{PCA}},\qquad
X_{\mathrm{embed+meta}}=[Z_{\mathrm{PCA}},\,M].
\]
Methods (RF outcome model, $B{=}150$, $N_{\mathrm{perm}}{=}120$,
$n_{\mathrm{doc}}{=}120$, $\alpha{=}0.05$):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RFPerm}: OOB vs.\ new predictive-risk gap.
  \item \textbf{DRPerm}: PO-risk
    $\mathbb{E}[\tau(X)^2]$ with
    $\tau(X)\approx\bigl(Y-\mu(X)\bigr)\bigl(W-e(X)\bigr)$.
  \item \textbf{CFPerm}: LOCO on the meta block (vs.\ trailing-PCA placebo),
    importance $R(-M)-R(\mathrm{full})$ with
    $R=\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\mu_1-\mu_0)^2\bigr]$.
\end{itemize}
Scenarios: \texttt{null\_shift} (Type-I);
\texttt{sentiment\_meta} (covariate routing on VADER compound);
\texttt{train\_vs\_heldout} (strong temporal/order split).

\noindent\textbf{$\Delta$ power (embed+meta $-$ embed):}
\texttt{sentiment\_meta}: RFPerm $+0.06$, DRPerm $0$, CFPerm $+0.01$;
\texttt{train\_vs\_heldout}: RF/DR $0$ (ceiling), CFPerm $\mathbf{+0.23}$.

\section{PO-risk reading}
Under pure covariate sentiment routing, mean PO-risk shrinks
$0.0054\to 0.0014$ when $M$ is added and DRPerm power stays $0$.
Thus $M$ acts as a better nuisance ($\mu,e$), not a louder alarm.
On \texttt{train\_vs\_heldout}, RF/DR are saturated at power $1$;
incremental value appears in \textbf{CFPerm localization}
($0.77\to 0.99$).

\section{Other covariate shifts (feature-only)}
A lighter study compares surface baseline vs.\ meta on planted
covariate alternatives (Type-I controlled at $0.05$):
\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Feature-only power: baseline vs.\ meta.}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Covariate scenario & Baseline & Meta \\
\midrule
sentiment\_shift (polarity; length matched) & 0.55 & \textbf{1.00} ($\Delta{+}0.45$) \\
syntax\_shift (POS mix; length matched) & 0.05 & \textbf{1.00} ($\Delta{+}0.95$) \\
entity\_shift (NER density) & 1.00 & 1.00 \\
style\_shift (length / punctuation) & 1.00 & 1.00 \\
null\_shift & 0.05 & 0.05 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

When the shift lives in polarity or syntax structure that baseline
cannot see, meta recovers near-perfect power. When the shift is already
visible to crude surface cues (entities via capitals; style via length),
baseline saturates and meta adds no further detection power.

\section{Takeaway}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Meta-features are calibrated (Type-I $\approx\alpha$).
  \item On covariate channels aligned with $M$, meta beats baseline
        (sentiment $+0.45$, syntax $+0.95$).
  \item On top of strong embeddings, do not expect DRPerm $\Delta$-power
        gains for pure covariate shift; watch PO-risk shrinkage and
        CFPerm block localization ($+0.23$ on heldout).
  \item Statistics value-add: explain whether a rejection is concept vs.\
        covariate, and allocate localization / review budget via CFPerm
        and meta blocks---not only raise raw detection rates.
\end{enumerate}
