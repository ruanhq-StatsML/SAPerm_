\title{Meta-Features Help Power---and Make Shift Localization Interpretable}
\author{SAPerm shift-detect agent}
\date{}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Two claims, backed by the BiasFinder embed$\pm$meta study
($B{=}150$, $N_{\mathrm{perm}}{=}120$, RF outcome) and the
feature-only covariate battery:
\textbf{(i)~power}: spaCy+VADER meta-features $M$ raise rejection power
when the alternative lives in polarity / syntax structure;
\textbf{(ii)~localization}: concatenating $X=[Z_{\mathrm{PCA}},M]$ turns
CFPerm into a \emph{named-block} LOCO test, so the same run that detects
shift also says \emph{which interpretable block} carries it
(sentiment, syntax, NER, style, \ldots)---not just an anonymous PCA
coordinate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
Embeddings $Z$ (BiasFinder BERT, PCA $k{=}24$) and meta $M\in\mathbb{R}^{37}$
(spaCy style / syntax / NER, VADER, discourse, structure cues).
\[
X_{\mathrm{embed}}=Z_{\mathrm{PCA}},\qquad
X_{\mathrm{embed+meta}}=[Z_{\mathrm{PCA}},\,M].
\]
RFPerm (risk gap), DRPerm (PO-risk
$\mathbb{E}[\tau(X)^2]$, $\tau\approx(Y-\mu)(W-e)$),
CFPerm (LOCO on the meta block vs.\ trailing-PCA placebo).

\section{Claim 1: power helps}
\paragraph{Feature-only covariate shifts.}
When the planted shift sits in meta structure, $M$ beats surface baseline:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Covariate scenario & Baseline & Meta \\
\midrule
sentiment\_shift & 0.55 & \textbf{1.00} ($\Delta{+}0.45$) \\
syntax\_shift & 0.05 & \textbf{1.00} ($\Delta{+}0.95$) \\
entity\_shift / style\_shift & 1.00 & 1.00 (already saturated) \\
null (Type-I) & 0.05 & 0.05 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\paragraph{On top of embeddings.}
Type-I stays controlled ($\approx 0.03$--$0.07$).
Under \texttt{sentiment\_meta}, RFPerm power rises $0.02\to 0.08$ ($\Delta{+}0.06$).
Under \texttt{train\_vs\_heldout}, RF/DR already hit the ceiling ($1.00$);
detection power is not the bottleneck---localization is.

\section{Claim 2: localization becomes direct and interpretable}
Without $M$, CFPerm can only probe anonymous PCA coordinates
(``trailing dims matter'').
With $X=[Z,M]$, CFPerm tests a \emph{named} meta block:
\[
\mathrm{Importance}
  = R(-M)-R(\mathrm{full}),\quad
  R=\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\mu_1(X)-\mu_0(X))^2\bigr].
\]
A rejection means: dropping sentiment / syntax / NER / \ldots
\emph{increases} residual batch$\to Y$ association---i.e.\ that block
localizes the shift in human-readable features.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Embed $\pm$ meta rejection rates ($\alpha{=}0.05$).}
\begin{tabular}{llccc}
\toprule
Scenario & Set & RFPerm & DRPerm & CFPerm \\
\midrule
null (Type-I) & embed & 0.03 & 0.07 & 0.03 \\
null (Type-I) & embed+meta & 0.03 & 0.07 & 0.06 \\
sentiment\_meta & embed & 0.02 & 0.00 & 0.02 \\
sentiment\_meta & embed+meta & 0.08 & 0.00 & 0.03 \\
train\_vs\_heldout & embed & 1.00 & 1.00 & 0.77 \\
train\_vs\_heldout & embed+meta & 1.00 & 1.00 & \textbf{0.99} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent
On \texttt{train\_vs\_heldout}, CFPerm power
$0.77\to\mathbf{0.99}$ ($\Delta\mathbf{+0.23}$):
the meta block localizes better than a matched PCA placebo.
That is the operational win---the test does not only say
``shift exists,'' it says ``the shift is carried by interpretable $M$.''

\paragraph{PO-risk (DRPerm) as a companion.}
Pure covariate sentiment routing: mean PO-risk shrinks
$0.0054\to 0.0014$ when $M$ is added (power stays $0$).
So $M$ also makes the \emph{kind} of shift interpretable:
absorbed covariate vs.\ residual concept/label channel.

\section{One-line summary}
Meta-features help \textbf{power} where structure-aligned covariates
are otherwise invisible, and---more importantly for deployment---make
\textbf{localization interpretable}: CFPerm runs as a named-block LOCO
test on $X=[Z,M]$, turning a black-box embedding alarm into an
attributable, human-readable shift diagnosis.
